## Verdict

Good commit. It is disciplined, fail-closed, and it closes four of the concrete defects
raised in the previous audit — including two I had flagged as unaddressed. Every
documented claim I spot-checked was true in the code, which is not the norm.

Two things temper that. First, one real design weakness remains in the measured-cost
path (§F1 below) that runs against the codebase's own fail-closed principle. Second,
and more important: **this commit does not change the headline operational state.**
Stressed-cost calibration is still disabled in the shipped configuration, so production
still requires an 83.33 bps stop against a grammar floor of 30 bps, and therefore still
refuses 100% of its own signals. That is a deliberate, documented choice — but it means
the system remains unable to place a trade.

## Defects closed since the previous audit

**C1 — Post-hoc repricing was a genuine causality bug, and it is now gone.**
The deleted `_reprice_with_schedule` docstring admitted the flaw itself: `simulate_account`
took *one* model for portfolio admission, then each realized opportunity was repriced with
its own symbol/time/depth cell afterwards. Refusals were therefore decided under one cost
model while P&L reflected another. Configured-versus-measured comparisons built on that
path were not measuring what they claimed. Replacing it with an in-simulation
`cost_resolver` is the correct fix, not a cosmetic one.

**C2 — Recorder cadence drift is fixed.** The loop moved from `time.sleep(args.interval)`
after the work — which made the true period `interval + work`, drifting continuously — to
a fixed monotonic tick:

```python
next_tick = _next_cadence_deadline(next_tick, now_monotonic, args.interval)
time.sleep(max(0.0, next_tick - time.monotonic()))
```

`_next_cadence_deadline` also handles overrun correctly, advancing to the first future
tick rather than firing a burst of catch-up requests. Combined with the 30 s interval,
this materially improves the odds of landing inside the ≤30 s quote-age window that
authorization requires. Realized cadence is now measured (p50/p95/gap) rather than assumed.

**C3 — SPY self-reference is now explicit.** Previously SPY benchmarked against itself,
producing an identically-zero residual and a silent structural no-op. It is now an
explicit `benchmark_self_reference` rejection.

**C4 — The residual family's own description was corrected.** The hypothesis text handed
to the LLM changed from "captures idiosyncratic directional flow" to "identifies
directional relative momentum; **this single-leg signal is not a beta-neutral or hedged
residual**." This matters more than it looks: the model reasons about families from this
text, and the previous wording invited it to treat a directional trade as market-neutral.

## Findings

### F1 — Measured-cost cell fallback is optimistic exactly where it should be conservative
**Severity: medium.** `research/quote_costs.py`, `_cell()`

Cell resolution degrades `symbol+bucket` → `symbol` → **universe aggregate**. Cells with
fewer than `min_quotes_per_cell` (default 500) quotes are correctly excluded from
publication — but the consequence of that exclusion is a fall-through to a universe-wide
spread pooled across all 24 symbols.

In this universe that pool spans roughly 0.17 bps (SPY, penny spread on a ~$600 price) to
2–3 bps (XLRE, SLV). So a symbol or half-hour cell with thin coverage inherits a spread
narrower than its own. The bias is not random: coverage is thinnest precisely where
quoting is sparsest, which is also where real spreads are widest — illiquid buckets near
the close, and the smaller members of the basket. The fallback therefore under-states
cost systematically in the worst cells.

The basis label (`"universe"`) is returned, so it is traceable in telemetry. But the
substitution is silent in the arithmetic, and it sits against the fail-closed principle
applied everywhere else in this commit. Two better options: fall back to the *widest*
measured cell rather than the mean, or refuse the cell and let the opportunity resolve to
the configured model with an explicit reason.

### F2 — `research/factory_report.py` silently requires Python 3.12+
**Severity: low (pre-existing, not introduced here).**

Line 1128 places a multi-line expression inside an f-string replacement field:

```python
f"; primary horizon {primary.get('horizon_minutes') or
primary.get('horizons')}m n={primary.get('candidate_count')}"
```

That is PEP 701 syntax, valid only on 3.12+. On 3.11 it is a `SyntaxError` at import, and
two test modules fail collection with a message that points at a string literal rather
than at a version mismatch.

The Dockerfile pins `python:3.13.5-slim-bookworm` and CI runs 3.13, so this passes
everywhere it actually runs. But the repository declares no `requires-python` floor
anywhere, and this is the only file in the tree carrying the requirement — I checked every
`.py` file at both `79553f7` and `d441736`. Introduced in `79553f7`, so it is inherited by
this commit rather than caused by it. Worth either a one-line reformat or an explicit
declared floor; the current failure mode is needlessly confusing.

### F3 — Beta is still not modeled; the allowlist is a blunter instrument
**Severity: informational — a deliberate design choice, noted for the record.**

The previous audit offered two remedies for the residual family's beta problem: model beta
per symbol, or restrict the family to comparable names. This commit took the second, and
the in-code comment states the reasoning honestly.

That is defensible and it removes the egregious cases. Two residuals remain inside the
eligible set:

- **XLU** (β ≈ 0.5 to SPY). `symbol_return − benchmark_return` still over-subtracts the
  market for a defensive sector; the "residual" retains a systematic short-SPY tilt.
- **EFA / EEM** are international. They trade US hours but track foreign markets, so their
  intraday relationship to SPY is dominated by overnight gaps and FX rather than by a
  contemporaneous beta. A one-minute residual against SPY is a noisier construct for these
  than for a domestic sector SPDR.

These are far milder than TLT or GLD were. Flagging them as candidates for the next
narrowing pass, or as the first argument for a rolling beta term.

### F4 — The trading blocker is unchanged, and that is the headline
**Severity: high — but deliberate and documented, not a defect.**

`stressed_cost_scenario_bps` remains 25.0 and `max_stressed_cost_to_risk_ratio` remains
0.30, so the admission gate still requires a stop of `25 / 0.30 = 83.33` bps of price
against a grammar floor of 30 bps. The calibrated 9 bps rung — which would require exactly
30 bps and open the window — is reachable only through the new shadow-only switch, and
both `config.yaml` and the compose default (`ALPACA_SHADOW_STRESS_CALIBRATION_ENABLED:-0`)
leave it off.

This commit makes the switch *explicit and safe* rather than *enabled*, which is the right
sequencing and matches the previous audit's recommendation to prove it in shadow first.
The point of recording it here is only that no one should read "P0-P3 remediation
complete" as "the system can now trade." It cannot, by design, until that switch is
exercised in shadow and the evidence supports promoting it.

### F5 — Empty commit body on a 31-file policy change
**Severity: low, process.**

`d441736` and `79553f7` both carry a subject line and no body. The two commits before them
(`a42348d`, `3c267d5`) had genuinely excellent bodies — stating the defect, the measured
magnitude, the fix, and the schema consequence. That standard is worth holding: this
commit changes admission policy, deletes a repricing path, and alters grammar eligibility,
and the reasoning now lives only in a separate doc that a future `git log` reader will not
see. The doc is good; the commit should carry at least a summary of it.
