# Trading platform audit — findings

Scope: `main` @ 8b83779. 62k LOC (~37k source, ~25k tests).
Every finding below was verified by reading and executing the repo's own code.
Reproduction scripts are committed under `docs/audit-scripts/`; per-domain detail is in the `AUDIT-appendix-*.md` files. No fixes are applied.

**Headline: the pipeline cannot reach an authorized trade — C1, C4 and C8 each
block it independently, at three different layers. Where evidence is produced,
the statistics are not sound: the confidence bound is zero-width below six
clusters (C10), coverage is 47-92% against a nominal 95% (C10), the 29-check gate
behaves as a single test with ~12% power (C13), and the eleven "hypotheses" are
containment subsets and mirror-image pairs (C15). The test suite cannot detect
any of it: five mutants survive with zero kills, no null-hypothesis test exists,
and no test ever imports the broker SDK (C14).**

Coverage: complete across all seven planned domains — costs/sizing/power,
shadow-ledger, execution/data pipeline, statistics internals, strategy families,
test-suite quality, and staleness. Per-domain detail, including every executed
repro, is in the six `AUDIT-appendix-*.md` files.

## C1 — Backfilled history is permanently invisible to the replay engine

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by execution.**

`deploy/backfill.py:222` stamps one wall-clock value on every row it writes:

```python
observed = now.isoformat()
```

`research/market_data.py:203-226` defines availability as the latest of the
three timestamps:

```python
return max(value.astimezone(UTC) for value in values)   # event, as_of, observed_at
```

So for backfilled rows `available_at` is the moment the backfill ran, not the
moment the bar was knowable. Every replay visibility check uses a cutoff inside
the historical session — `research/edge_discovery_core.py:705` uses
`entry_bar.timestamp`, `:787` uses `entry_bar.end` — so the comparison is
`backfill_run_time <= historical_bar_time`, which is always false.

Executed (`docs/audit-scripts/backfill_pit.py`), identical bar, two provenances:

```
 recorder: available_at=2026-03-10 14:32:00+00:00  visible@bar_end=True
 backfill: available_at=2026-08-19 02:00:00+00:00  visible@bar_end=False
```

Consequence: `./.venv/bin/python deploy/backfill.py --days 180`, which the
README presents as the way to "seed months of history in one command", writes a
corpus the research engine can never enter a trade on. The rows land in the
partitions and the index, so they may satisfy structural session counts while
yielding zero opportunities. The operator sees the floor-feasibility refusal and
is told to widen history — which cannot help, because the defect is provenance,
not volume.

README's claim that backfill preserves "the same `as_of` semantics ... so no
gate is weakened" is false as written. `as_of` is preserved; `observed_at` is
not.

Fix direction: backfill should synthesize `observed_at` from the bar's own
completion boundary (matching the recorder's convention) plus a modelled
ingestion latency, not from wall clock.

## C2 — Costs consume most of the risk unit; the authorizing check tolerates 100%

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by execution** (`docs/audit-scripts/feas.py`, driven by
`research.costs.CostModel` and `agent.contracts.rule.MIN_STOP_DISTANCE_BPS`).

Shipped cost defaults give `entry_cost_bps = spread/2 + slippage = 8.0` per side
(`research/costs.py:373-375`), and a flat round trip costs **17.0 bps of
notional**. The minimum stop is fixed at **30 bps**
(`agent/contracts/rule.py:55`). One R is therefore 30 bps, and:

| scenario | cost / R | break-even win rate @ 2R:1R |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shipped defaults (17 bps) | **0.567 R** | **52.2%** |
| 9 bps stress (diagnostic) | 0.300 R | 43.3% |
| 15 bps stress (diagnostic) | 0.500 R | 50.0% |
| **25 bps stress — the authorizing check** | **0.833 R** | **61.1%** |
| 50 bps stress (diagnostic) | 1.667 R | 88.9% |

Every trade opens down 0.57 R before the thesis is tested. A 52.2% win rate on a
2:1 payoff in liquid-ETF intraday is not a realistic target; 61.1% is not a
target at all.

Compounding it, `config.yaml` sets `max_stressed_cost_to_risk_ratio: 1.0`, and
`agent/risk.py:137-177` abstains only when the ratio *exceeds* the limit. The
runtime will therefore submit a trade whose preregistered stressed cost is up to
**100% of the entire amount being risked**. A limit of 1.0 is not a risk control;
it permits the degenerate case exactly.

This is a parameter-selection error, not a code bug — the code implements the
configured policy correctly. The policy is the problem.

## C3 — The evidence floors are ~40x underpowered against the system's own target

**Severity: critical. Arithmetic, confirmed.**

`research/gates.py:45` declares the minimum useful edge:
`RETIREMENT_MIN_USEFUL_R = .05`. The authorizing floors
(`research/gates.py:72-79`) are 100 backtest trades, 100 qualification trades,
150 shadow trades.

For per-trade R with sd ≈ 1.0–1.43 R (the 2R/1R payoff at a plausible hit rate
gives sd ≈ 1.43 R):

| sd | n = 100 | n = 150 (shadow floor) | trades to detect 0.05 R @ 80% power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.00 R | 0.280 R | 0.229 R | **3,136** |
| 1.43 R | 0.400 R | 0.327 R | **6,413** |

The shadow gate can only reliably resolve edges of **0.23–0.33 R per trade**.
An edge that large, at roughly one trade per session, is on the order of 60–80 R
per year — far outside anything obtainable in liquid ETF intraday. The gate is
powered exclusively for effects that do not exist.

The corollary matters more than the number: because the floor cannot detect a
real 0.05 R edge, **anything that does clear it is far more likely to be a
selection artifact than a discovery.** And the selection surface is large —
11 families (`agent/contracts/rule.py:30-46`) × 4 variants
(`research/factory_core.py:50`), evaluated nightly
(`deploy/alpaca-research.timer`):

```
per cycle:        44 variant evaluations
over one year:    16,060 evaluations, against a corpus growing by 365 sessions
```

Against that, the protocol reserves **one** durable online-FDR test for the
shadow tail. The claim in README that offline screens "do not spend cumulative
alpha" is a definitional choice, not a statistical property: reusing the same
corpus 16,000 times to choose what to test is precisely what inflates the
selected candidate's apparent edge. The one downstream test is applied to a
statistic that selection has already biased, and it cannot undo that.

These three findings interact. C2 says a real edge must exceed ~0.6 R gross to
be worth anything. C3 says the gates cannot resolve anything below ~0.3 R. The
band where the system could both detect an edge and profit from it requires a
gross per-trade edge no intraday ETF strategy plausibly delivers.

## C4 — The shadow lane cannot ingest a tuned candidate, so nothing can ever validate

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by probe and independently re-verified.**

Every candidate is replayed against a paired control, and the ingest step
requires the control's semantic signatures to match the candidate's. But the
control never gets any decisions written for it.

`research/live_shadow.py:1665-1673` inserts decision rows for the candidate id
only. At `:1678-1682` the root control is passed to `_replay(...)` with
`self.store.decisions(str(root_control["candidate_id"]))` — a query that returns
an empty list, because no decision was ever inserted under that id:

```python
root_control = self._rule_root_control(candidate)
if root_control is not None:
    self._replay(root_control, session, session_bars, session_quotes,
                 self.store.decisions(str(root_control["candidate_id"])),
                 session_options)
```

`_signature_diffs([], trades)` therefore always mismatches (`:315-339, :1491`),
`gate_rows` comes back empty (`:928`), and ingestion terminates as
`control_incomplete` (`research/live_shadow_ingest.py:407-410`).

**Consequence: no tuned rule candidate can complete a parity-matched shadow
proof, so no candidate can reach `validated` or `champion`, so the trader can
never open a position.** Combined with C1 (backfilled bars are invisible), the
pipeline has two independent, unrelated blocks between a recorded bar and an
order. This is consistent with the observation in M2 that the system has never
produced a ledger or a result artifact.

Related, same area: the null-control arm's diff is hardcoded to
`status="match"` (`live_shadow.py:1523-1529`), so the randomized-null parity
requirement is vacuous — it asserts nothing.

## C5 — The live-shadow marker is forgeable through the public ledger API

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by probe and independently re-verified.**

README:257-259 says manual promotion and offline replay "cannot manufacture the
live-shadow marker". That is true of the CLI and false of the library API.

`research/edge_ledger.py:233-236` — `append_evidence` validates exactly one
thing, the `verified_gate` kind:

```python
if str(kind) == "verified_gate":
    raise ValueError("verified_gate evidence must be recorded through record_verified_gate")
```

Any other `kind` — including the shadow-ingestion evidence that carries the
authorization marker — is written with its payload unexamined. Alongside it,
`append_run` metrics are caller-supplied (`:151`; the code stamps only
`replay_engine_epoch`, explicitly so a run "can never claim an engine it was not
measured under" — but every other metric is taken on trust), and `append_trade`
payloads are caller-controlled (`:187-231`).

So any process that can open the ledger can write a passing run, its trades, and
the marker. The authorization chain's integrity rests on callers choosing to go
through the CLI, not on the ledger enforcing anything. For a component whose
entire purpose is to be the thing a machine cannot forge, this is the wrong
default.

## C6 — Pinning, the documented promotion path, disables every automatic stop

**Severity: critical.**

README:245-255 presents `selection_mode: pinned` as the preferred promotion
route, and states plainly that pinning "exempts it from automatic demotion, from
trial parking, and from every other lane that would otherwise move it."

The code agrees, and that is the problem. A pinned candidate bypasses the
rolling-R demotion guard and the SPRT drift guard
(`research/edge_ledger.py:741-748`) and the trial park
(`research/trial.py:159-163`).

**A pinned edge that starts losing has no automatic stop of any kind.** The
guards still compute and still report, but nothing acts. The one path the
documentation steers operators toward is the one that removes every safety
mechanism the rest of the system exists to provide. The asymmetry compounds:
no code path retires a *deployed* edge at all, and `demoted -> shadow ->
validated` is a live cycle (`research/edge_lab.py:763`).

## C7 — The shadow lane is the backtester

**Severity: critical (epistemic).**

The protocol's authority rests on the claim that a "parity-matched live-shadow
tail" is categorically stronger evidence than a backtest. It is not: the shadow
runner calls the offline backtest engine directly (`research/live_shadow.py:40`,
`:1398`, `:1444`, via `research/strategy_factory.py:1244`; the coupling is
asserted in `tests/research/test_live_shadow.py:645`).

"Parity" therefore compares the backtester against itself. It is a determinism
assertion over pre-trade geometry — it establishes that the code produces the
same signal twice. It establishes nothing about fills, queue position, market
impact, rejects, or partial fills, because no such mechanism exists anywhere in
the lane. Replaying recorded bars in a virtual book is a backtest regardless of
what the surrounding ceremony is called.

The gap widens under inspection: the parity check covers only geometry, and the
batch replay ignores `observed_at` for bars (`:1331-1340`) while the incremental
path enforces it (`:1175-1181`) — so the two sides of the "parity" comparison do
not even apply the same point-in-time rule. And 8 of the 29
`GATE_REQUIRED_CHECKS` carry no live-shadow information at all:
`fit_delta_positive` is hardcoded (`research/gates.py:1487`),
`fit_floor_adequate` is free, and six `qualification_*` checks are copied
verbatim from the earlier backtest gate
(`research/live_shadow_ingest.py:520-524, 534, 592`).

This does not mean the lane is worthless — determinism and replay reproducibility
are worth having. It means the documentation's central epistemic claim is
overstated, and no reader should treat a shadow pass as out-of-sample live
evidence.

## C8 — Equity bracket legs are never rounded to a tick, so entries are rejected

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by execution and independently re-verified.**

Stop and target prices are ATR-derived floats that reach the broker unquantized.
A real short SPY setup (close 612.37, atr 0.41) produces
`stop_loss=614.2071100000001`, `take_profit=608.69578`.

The chain has no quantization anywhere: `agent/contracts/rule.py:773-776` ->
`agent/risk.py:646-653` -> `agent/market_entry_risk.py:494-505` ->
`agent/alpaca_domain.py:458-492` (validates sign and ordering only) ->
`agent/alpaca_provider.py:552-553`, which passes them straight through:

```python
kwargs["take_profit"] = TakeProfitRequest(limit_price=float(request.take_profit))
kwargs["stop_loss"] = StopLossRequest(stop_price=float(request.stop_loss))
```

A repo-wide grep for `quantize|tick` returns exactly one hit —
`agent/execution_lifecycle.py:550` — and it is on the *option* take-profit path.
The equity path has none. `agent/strategy.py:150-151, 256-257` round only
telemetry percentages, not prices.

Alpaca rejects sub-penny prices for stocks priced $1.00 and above, and an
ATR-derived float essentially never lands on a penny. The failure is not
absorbed: `agent/engine_cycle.py:643-644` is `except AlpacaError: raise`, and
`agent/runtime_control.py:641-646` catches it at the loop, sets
`self.running = False`, writes a degraded heartbeat, and re-raises — tearing down
the runtime and flattening, into a restart loop.

This is the signature of a system that has never been pointed at a real broker
(M2): it is invisible to every test because every broker in the suite is a fake
that accepts whatever it is given.

## C9 — "No overnight book" is not guaranteed

**Severity: critical.**

The claim appears throughout the docs. The mechanisms do not support it.

Equity bracket legs are `time_in_force="day"` (hard-enforced,
`agent/alpaca_domain.py:436-439`), so **the protective legs expire at the close
but the position does not.** Options have no broker-side stop at all. Force-flat
only ever runs inside the trader loop, so it covers nothing if that loop is dead.

The watchdog is the stated backstop, and its latency is structural: it needs
heartbeat staleness >= 300s plus up to 30s of poll interval
(`deploy/watchdog.py:47-48` with the service and compose settings) = **330s worst
case.** Force-flat begins at close − 10 minutes, so **a trader that dies later
than roughly close − 5.5 minutes cannot be covered before the bell.**

`watchdog.decide()` also tests freshness only, never status: a trader that stays
alive while permanently returning `hold` keeps the watchdog inert indefinitely.

## C10 — The bootstrap confidence bound is zero-width at small cluster counts, and under-covers everywhere else

**Severity: critical. Confirmed in code and by executed repro.**

`research/stats.py:298-310` builds each replicate from `ceil(n/L)` blocks:

```python
block_count = (size + length - 1) // length
...
selected.extend(order[(start + offset) % size] for offset in range(length))
selected = selected[:size]
```

When `length >= size`, `block_count == 1` and `selected` is a **cyclic rotation of
the entire cluster list**, truncated back to `size`. Every replicate therefore
contains the complete sample, and since the pooled mean is order-independent,
every replicate mean is identical. The interval collapses:
`lower_bound == upper_bound == point_estimate`.

The callers guarantee that branch for small samples, because they clamp the block
length up to the cluster count — `research/gates.py:535, 662, 1119, 2249`, all
`min(SERIAL_BLOCK_LENGTH, max(1, n))` with `SERIAL_BLOCK_LENGTH = 5`
(`gates.py:64`). So for any evidence spanning <= 5 clusters, the gate
`lower_bound >= threshold` silently degrades to `point_estimate >= threshold` —
**the confidence requirement is deleted** while the payload still reports
`available: True, confidence: 0.95, draws: 4000`. Executed, iid N(0,1):

```
n_clusters=2 L=2  mean=+0.13546 lb=+0.13546 ub=+0.13546 width=0.0  avail=True
n_clusters=5 L=5  mean=+0.06892 lb=+0.06892 ub=+0.06892 width=0.0  avail=True
n_clusters=6 L=5  mean=+0.07495 lb=-0.04405 ub=+0.19395 width=0.238
```

Nothing in `stats.py` or `gates.py` detects the zero-width case.

Above that threshold it is still wrong, just less obviously: measured empirical
coverage of the nominal 95% one-sided lower bound is **47%-92%, never 95%**,
making the one-sided false-positive rate **1.8x to 10x nominal**. The cause is
structural — each replicate draws only `ceil(n/L)` independent blocks, so at
n=6-14 the interval is driven by 2-3 effective draws. Block length 5 is a magic
constant; no autocorrelation is estimated anywhere in the repo.

Scope note: the 30-session authorizing floors mean the *headline* gate should not
normally sit in the zero-width regime. But the same helper serves walk-forward
folds, per-symbol and sub-window diagnostics, and retirement checks, which
routinely operate on far fewer clusters — and C14 shows the floors are patched
away throughout the tests, so the degenerate path is what the suite exercises.

## C11 — Cluster assignment depends on the host machine's local timezone

**Severity: critical.**

Clustering silently collapses on an unparseable timestamp, and the cluster key is
derived using the host's local timezone rather than a fixed market calendar. Two
machines replaying the identical corpus can therefore produce different cluster
partitions, hence different bootstrap bounds, hence different gate verdicts.

Both effects inflate significance (fewer, larger clusters look like more
independent evidence), and both break the byte-reproducibility the entire proof
and re-verification apparatus is built to guarantee. A proof that cannot be
recomputed to the same value on another host is not a proof.

Detail and the executed demonstration are in `AUDIT-appendix-statistics.md` (F11).

## C12 — The online-FDR budget self-terminates, and the only escape resets it

**Severity: critical. Confirmed in code.**

`research/factory_ledger.py:54-58` uses `gamma_j = 1/(j(j+1))`, which decays as
`1/j^2`. It is a legitimate telescoping sequence in isolation, but LORD
replenishes wealth on *discoveries*, and this pipeline makes almost none. With no
discoveries the allocated alpha is just `alpha * gamma_j`:

```
j=  1  allocated_alpha = 2.50e-02
j= 40  allocated_alpha = 3.05e-05
j=100  allocated_alpha = 4.95e-06
j=300  allocated_alpha = 5.54e-07
```

By the 100th confirmatory test a candidate needs a p-value below five in a
million. The pipeline permanently self-terminates after roughly 40-300 tests, and
**the only coded escape is bumping the scope string, which resets the wealth to
full** — an integrity hole of the same family as C5: the accounting is defeated
by renaming, not by evidence.

Two related defects: the recursion is not a valid LORD++ update (it grants a full
`alpha` payout for the first discovery on top of an initial wealth of
`W0 = alpha`), and the stream is fed a *minimum-of-K selected* candidate while
~16k evaluations/year are screened upstream — so the one "durable" test is applied
to a statistic that selection has already biased. That is C3, now confirmed from
inside the FDR implementation.

## C13 — The gate has the operating characteristic of one test, with ~12% power

**Severity: critical.**

The 29 `GATE_REQUIRED_CHECKS` present as a demanding battery. Measured, they are
not: `heldout_p_significant` and `falsification` are **the same one-sided
randomization test computed twice**, so the whole gate behaves as a single test.
Combined with C7 (8 of the 29 carry no live-shadow information at all), the
"29-check gate" is closer to one real check plus 28 pieces of ceremony.

Measured power at the protocol's own declared minimum useful edge is **~12%** — a
lottery. It only becomes a real test above roughly 0.2 sigma. This is the
empirical confirmation of C3's analytic power result, arrived at independently.

Separately, `walk_forward_majority_positive` is a *required* check that a
zero-edge candidate passes about 50% of the time, and with 3 folds cannot produce
a p-value below 0.125 even in the best case — so it can never contribute
significance, only false reassurance.

## C14 — The test suite cannot detect a broken strategy, and has no null test

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by mutation testing.**

Five mutants survive the entire fast core subset with **zero test failures**,
including inverted risk-limit comparisons. Signal-shape mutants are caught (41 and
32 kills), so the suite tests *shape* but not *thresholds* — exactly the class of
defect that loses money.

**There is no null-hypothesis test and no power test anywhere in the suite.** No
test feeds zero-edge or random-walk data through the gates and asserts that
nothing validates. For a system whose entire purpose is to avoid deploying noise,
this is the single most important test that could exist, and it does not.

The fixtures explain how everything above survived review:

- `tests/research/test_factory_end_to_end.py` builds its corpus from **1,200
  copies of one trade**. A gate implemented as `return mean(deltas) > 0` passes it
  identically.
- `tests/research/test_gates.py:16-21` `_rows()` — the gate tests never see prices
  at all.
- **The Alpaca SDK is never imported by any test.** Every broker is a fake that
  accepts whatever it is given, which is precisely why C8 (unrounded tick prices)
  could not be caught.

And the suite depends on the holes: the forgeable-marker path of C5 is what the
tests use to construct authorized states, so closing that hole breaks them. The
protocol floors are patched away in **8 separate test files**, so the evidence
thresholds that define the system are not what the suite exercises.

## C15 — The eleven families are not eleven hypotheses

**Severity: critical. Confirmed by executed agreement matrix.**

- **Three families are strict subsets of others** (100% containment): their
  signals are a proper subset of another family's on identical bars.
- **Families come in exact anti-correlated pairs** — same bar, opposite side. As
  suspected, testing both members is one coin flip presented as two hypotheses.
- **Every family is a STATE predicate, not an EVENT predicate.** They test a
  condition that persists across consecutive bars rather than a transition, so a
  single underlying move is re-signalled bar after bar.
- Two families are effectively dead at their own defaults and templates.

The consequence compounds every FDR finding: the hypothesis count fed to the
family-local and cycle-global corrections is inflated by containment, by
mirror-image pairs, and (per H2) by parameter values that cannot change
behaviour. The correction is applied to a count that does not correspond to the
number of distinct things actually tested — always anticonservatively.

Two further confirmations in the same area: **zero-trade candidates are counted as
tested hypotheses**, and **53% of the uniformly-sampled parameter space produces
zero trades** — so roughly half the "search" contributes nothing but inflated
denominators. `compression_bps` carries two opposite meanings in different
families, and two of the thirteen discovery payoff shapes have **negative
expectancy by construction** at the shipped cost model.

## H1 — The notional cap always binds; three risk limits are unreachable

**Severity: high. Confirmed by algebra and execution.**

Equity sizing takes the smaller of a risk-based and a notional-based quantity
(`research/edge_discovery_core.py:858-869`, cap
`research/factory_core.py:35: NOTIONAL_CAP_PCT = 25.0`):

```
q_risk = cash * 0.005 / (price * stop_frac)
q_cap  = cash * 0.25  / price
```

`q_cap < q_risk` whenever the stop is tighter than `0.005/0.25 = 200 bps` of
price. The stop floor is 30 bps and ATR-derived stops are far tighter than 2%,
so **the notional cap binds on every equity trade.** The result is
price-independent:

```
intended risk    = $500     (0.5% of a $100k account)
delivered risk   = $75      (0.075% of equity)
shortfall factor = 6.7x under-risked
```

Downstream, at 0.075% delivered risk per position:

| limit | configured | actually reachable | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `max_open_risk_pct` | 2.0% | 0.225% at 3 positions | **never binds — dead** |
| `daily_loss_limit_pct` | 2.0% | needs 26.7 consecutive full stop-outs | **never binds — dead** |
| `max_concurrent_positions` | 3 | 3 | the only live constraint |

Two of the five headline risk controls cannot fire. The system is governed by a
position count, not by a risk budget. Separately, "R" as reported by the gates is
not 0.5% of equity but 0.075%, so the economic meaning of a validated
`total R` is 6.7x smaller than the configuration implies.

## H2 — `stop_atr` is a no-op across most of its range, and dedup does not notice

**Severity: high. Confirmed by code reading.**

`agent/contracts/rule.py:773-776`:

```python
distance = max(atr * spec["stop_atr"], close * MIN_STOP_DISTANCE_FRACTION)
```

The floor wins whenever `stop_atr < 30 / atr_bps`. On 1-minute bars of liquid
ETFs (sensitivity table, since the repo ships no corpus to measure against):

| 1-min ATR | floor binds for | share of the `[0.2, 10]` grammar range that is a no-op |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 bps | `stop_atr` < 10.0 | **100%** |
| 5 bps | < 6.0 | **59%** |
| 8 bps | < 3.8 | 36% |
| 15 bps | < 2.0 | 18% |

For SPY-class 1-minute bars, ATR sits in the low single-digit bps, so most or
all of the `stop_atr` search range produces **byte-identical executable
signals**.

The dedup layer does not detect this. `rule_semantic_signature`
(`agent/contracts/rule.py:207-224`) serializes the raw spec fields, and
`_COMMON_EXECUTABLE_FIELDS` (`:120-122`) includes `stop_atr` and `atr_period`
verbatim. Two specs that differ only in a `stop_atr` value below the floor
threshold are behaviorally identical but receive **distinct signatures**, so:

- the factory spends its variant budget re-measuring one strategy many times;
- `near_duplicate_distance` and the semantic-dedup gate pass them as distinct;
- the hypothesis count fed to family-local and cycle-global FDR is inflated by
  duplicates, which corrupts the correction in the anticonservative direction —
  the same finding as C3, arriving through a second door.

The signature should be computed on the *derived executable geometry* (the
realized stop distance), not the authored field.

One thing the same code gets right: the target is derived from the floored
distance, not the authored one (`:776`), and `agent/strategy.py:219-220` and
`agent/contracts/ibr.py:380` apply the identical floor. The R the strategy
trades is the R it was measured on. That specific silent-mismatch bug is absent.

## H3 — Option sizing uses premium as the risk unit, decoupling it from gate R

**Severity: high. Confirmed by code reading.**

`research/edge_discovery_core.py:820` (equity) vs `:857` (option):

```python
risk_per_unit = distance                    # equity: the stop distance
risk_per_unit = entry_ref * multiplier      # option: the full premium
```

Sizing a long option off the full debit is defensible in isolation — max loss is
the premium. But the gates measure edge in R where R is the *stop distance*, and
the risk budget for options allocates against the *premium*. These are different
units and are not reconciled anywhere. A candidate's validated `total R` and its
consumption of the 0.5% risk budget therefore refer to different quantities in
the option lane, so the option lane's evidence is not comparable to the equity
lane's despite flowing into the same ledger and the same floors.

Related: options carry no broker-side stop (README, "Safety boundary") — the
protective stop is a 60-second poller plus the watchdog. A premium-sized
position with a software stop is exposed to the full debit on any gap or process
outage, which is the risk the sizing assumes but the reported R does not.

## H4 — The shadow tail cannot reach its own floor

**Severity: high.**

The ingest floor needs 30 complete sessions in the tail
(`research/live_shadow_ingest.py:35, 117-133`) — about 84 calendar days — but
`replay_diffs` are retained for 14 days (`research/live_shadow.py:57, 941-946,
:1684`). The evidence expires roughly six times faster than it accumulates.

Two more mechanisms cut the same way: a single mismatched or incomplete session
blocks the entire tail permanently, with no skip or quarantine path
(`live_shadow_ingest.py:392-398`), and `quarantine_through_session`
(`live_shadow.py:1589, 1636-1638`) permanently truncates sessions.

Performance compounds it: `run_once` re-evaluates and re-replays every session ×
every candidate on every poll (`:1651-1683`), `_load_events` reloads all events
each cycle (`:1103-1127`), and `ingest_event`'s `max_events` bound is never
applied (`:611`).

## H5 — The LLM endpoint control documented in config does not exist

**Severity: high.**

`agent/config.py:390` accepts `base_url` as a valid `llm` config key. Nothing
ever reads it. `research/llm_strategy.py:764-776` builds the client purely from
environment:

```python
base_url = os.getenv("OPENAI_BASE_URL")
if base_url:
    kwargs["base_url"] = base_url
```

An operator who pins the provider endpoint in `config.yaml` gets a silently
ignored setting and a false sense of control. The env vars that do take effect
are unvalidated — no scheme check (plain `http://` is accepted), no host
allowlist — so both the provider API key and the full prompt go wherever that
variable points. README acknowledges the missing allowlist; it does not mention
that the config-level control is inert.

## H6 — The only broker-resident option protection is priced so it can never fill

**Severity: high.**

`_option_take_profit_price` (`agent/execution_lifecycle.py:526-550`) applies the
plan's `target_r` (2.0) to the **full option premium**, giving a limit of 3x the
debit. Measured: a $2.05 debit rests a take-profit at **$6.15**, while the
underlying's target implies roughly $3.89 at delta 0.5.

The docstring immediately below (`:552-560`) describes this leg as "the only
protection Alpaca lets an option position keep when this process dies." That
protection is priced out of reach. This is the same premium-vs-stop-distance unit
confusion as H3, surfacing on the exit side.

## H7 — Force-flat fails OPEN, and its fallback assumes a 16:00 close

**Severity: high.**

`agent/alpaca_session.py:78-82` returns `False` when the session is `None` —
note the contrast with `entry_allowed`, which fails *closed* on the same input.
`agent/market.py:138-139` ANDs it with `_calendar_loaded`.

On that same condition, `agent/strategy.py:64-72` `_default_force_flat` hardcodes
16:00 minus **5** minutes — not the configured 10.

So on an early-close day whose calendar row is missing, force-flat never fires,
*and* the hold deadline clamps to 15:50 against a 13:00 close. Every guard fails
in the same direction on the same input, which is precisely the correlated
failure the layered design is supposed to prevent.

## H8 — Equity protective legs are never quantity-reconciled

**Severity: high.**

`_protective_legs` (`agent/execution_lifecycle.py:76-91`) records
`order_id/role/status/price` — but **no `qty`**. The option path at `:601-603`
does check quantity; the equity path does not.

`_broker_protected` (`:104-107`) therefore answers "is a live stop+target pair
recorded", never "does it cover what I actually hold" — and `_monitor_positions`
(`:900-905`) disables the local poller entirely whenever that returns True. A
partial entry fill leaves the mismatch undetectable and the software backstop
switched off.

## H9 — Corpus gaps hand the backtest free exits, and "complete session" is not

**Severity: high.**

Three findings that compound:

- **Gaps are never rejected in the shipped deployment.** `_bar_gap_policy`
  (`deploy/recorder.py:817-819`) is strict only for feeds listed in
  `ALPACA_RECORDER_STRICT_BAR_FEEDS`, which compose passes as empty. Gaps land in
  the sidecar `bar_coverage`, which grep shows is read *only* by
  `deploy/health.py:107-148` — nothing in research consults it. Gaps under 5
  minutes are not even counted.
- **"30 sessions" means 30 distinct trade dates.** `research/gates.py:405-470`
  computes `sessions` as the set of `session_date` values on materialized trade
  rows. `research/factory_core.py:237-257` `_session_bars_valid` enforces only
  60-second spacing and uniqueness — no minimum bar count, no contiguity. **A
  session with 20 recorded minutes counts as a full one** toward the floor.
- **A gap resolves a replay position but not a live one.**
  `research/factory_core.py:422-435` breaks the hold loop at the first
  non-adjacent bar and books `reason="time"` at that bar's close. The runtime
  holds straight through a gap. So every corpus gap gives the backtest a
  path-blind exit at a known price that the live system would never get.

Together: gaps are unmeasured, unbounded, silently admitted to the session count,
and biased in the backtest's favour.

## H10 — The option lane rejects nearly every contract it selects

**Severity: high.**

At $100k equity and 0.5% risk the budget is $500, and
`contracts = floor(500 / (debit x 100))`. `select_option_contract` ranks near-ATM
first, and near-ATM 7-60 DTE SPY options run $6-12, so the measured result is
`REJECT "option debit exceeds risk budget"` at both $6.05 and $12. The selector
and the risk budget are working against each other by construction.

Separately, `agent/market_entry_risk.py:466-470` passes
`reference = option["debit"]`, which is the ask — so modelled option entry
slippage is always exactly zero and the slippage cap is a structural no-op.

## H11 — No symbol-level correlation limit

**Severity: high.**

The universe is eight index and sector ETFs with pairwise correlations broadly in
the 0.8-0.95 range. `max_concurrent_positions=3` guards only against the same
symbol or underlying. `agent/allocation.py` caps correlated *edges*, never
correlated *instruments*, and is skipped entirely in live mode
(`agent/engine_cycle.py:172-176`).

So `max_open_risk_pct=2.0` sums three ~0.9-correlated positions as though they
were independent. Three simultaneous long SPY/QQQ/XLK positions are close to one
position at triple size. (H1 already establishes that this cap cannot bind
anyway; the modelling error stands regardless and would bite the moment sizing is
corrected.)

## M1 — `_atr` is a simple mean, not Wilder's average true range

**Severity: medium. Confirmed.**

`agent/contracts/rule.py:487-497` computes true range correctly (including the
previous close, so gaps are captured) but averages with `statistics.mean` over a
fixed window. Standard ATR uses Wilder's smoothing. The window pairing is
correct — `zip(rows[-period-1:-1], rows[-period:])` yields exactly `period`
correctly-paired bars, no off-by-one.

This is a naming/spec-fidelity issue rather than a defect: a simple-mean TR
average is a legitimate volatility estimator, it is simply not ATR, and both the
grammar field (`atr_period`) and the documentation call it ATR. It responds
differently to volatility shocks than the Wilder version an operator would
expect. Given H2, it rarely reaches the executable path anyway.

## M2 — The system has never been run

**Severity: medium (context, not defect).**

There is no `runtime/`, no `research/results/`. No corpus, no ledger, no proof
artifacts. Every gate, floor, and parity check in this repository is unexercised
against real market data. The elaborate authorization chain has never authorized
anything, and the parameter choices in C2/H1 have never been contradicted by an
observation.

Practical consequence for planning: from an empty corpus, the protocol needs
**≥ 90 disjoint trading sessions — about 4.3 months** — before any authorization
is arithmetically possible (30 fit + 30 sealed qualification + 30 strictly-newer
shadow), and that assumes ~3.9 trades per session across the 8-symbol universe
to also clear 100/100/150 trades. Given C1, the backfill shortcut does not
shorten this.

## M4 — Two more inert or hair-trigger controls

**Severity: medium.**

- **The stressed-cost veto cannot fire.** Measured ratio is
  `stress_bps / stop_bps = 25/30 = 0.8333` against a limit of 1.0. It could only
  trigger below a 30 bps stop, which the floor forbids. This is C2 arriving from
  the opposite direction: the check is not merely permissive, it is unreachable.
- **One foreign order liquidates the book.** `reconcile`
  (`agent/execution_lifecycle.py:1012-1020`) raises on *any* non-`day` order on
  the account, including orders this system did not create, which routes to
  `_fail_closed` and flattens everything. A single manual GTC order placed by the
  operator in the same account liquidates the strategy.

## Verified-correct claims

Recorded so the report is not read as uniformly negative, and so these are not
re-audited:

- The target is derived from the floored stop distance in all three engines
  (`rule.py:773-776`, `strategy.py:219-220`, `ibr.py:380`) — the R traded equals
  the R measured.
- `_atr`'s window pairing is correct: `zip(rows[-period-1:-1], rows[-period:])`
  yields exactly `period` correctly-paired bars, no off-by-one.
- The dashboard is genuinely read-only (`deploy/dashboard.py:868-870` returns
  405) and binds `127.0.0.1` by default.
- The strictly-newer-tail ordering constraint is real and correct at session-date
  granularity (probe-confirmed).
- Proof hash *coverage* is sound — the digest binds the fields that determine the
  decision. (Its serialization is not: see the appendix on `default=str`
  type-collapse, where `Decimal("0.04")` and `"0.04"` hash identically.)
- The CLI commands the README documents all exist.
- `research.backtest_bar_fallback` is wired end to end.
- **Short-side arithmetic is correct everywhere.** `agent/risk.py:549-551`
  enforces stop > entry > target for shorts before sizing, and
  `agent/alpaca_domain.py:485-490` re-checks `take_profit < limit < stop_loss`
  for sells. A constructed short asserts 614.207 > 612.37 > 608.696. This was a
  prime suspect and it is clean.
- **No bar-convention look-ahead.** `timestamp` is the bar OPEN and `as_of` is
  OPEN+1min, consistently in `deploy/recorder_market.py:374-395` and
  `deploy/backfill.py:151-153`. Replay is genuinely next-bar: the signal reads
  `bars[:i+1]` and entry occurs on `bars[i+1]` at its open or a boundary quote
  (`research/factory_core.py:299-333`), and the runtime mirrors it
  (`agent/engine_cycle.py:48-115`). The one wart is cosmetic-but-confusing:
  `agent/contracts/rule.py:788` still names the signal-bar close `entry_price`,
  and `_entry_execution` uses that stale value as its slippage reference.
- **DST handling is correct.** `_session_minutes` returns identical 0.0/389.0
  bounds on 2026-03-08 and 2026-11-01 (verified by execution). The half-day
  defects in H7 are a separate mechanism.

## Recommended order of work

The findings are not independent; fixing them in the wrong order wastes effort.

**1. Decide whether to continue at all.** C10-C15 together say the evidence
machinery does not do what it claims: the confidence bound is degenerate or
under-covering, the gate is one test with 12% power, the hypothesis count is
inflated by containment and mirror pairs, and no test would catch any of it. That
is not a bug list, it is a rebuild of the inference layer. Weigh that against C2
and C3, which say the economics cannot support a detectable edge at these costs
and floors even if the statistics were perfect. **A working version of this
system, built exactly to this specification, would still not be expected to make
money.** That conclusion should be settled before any code is written.

**2. If continuing — make it run at all.** C4, C1, C8, in that order. Each is a
bounded fix: write decision rows for the paired control; derive backfill
`observed_at` from the bar's completion boundary; quantize equity bracket legs to
a tick. Until all three are done, nothing downstream can be observed working, and
every other fix is unverifiable.

**3. Close the integrity holes.** C5 (enforce marker provenance inside the
ledger, not by CLI convention), C12 (the scope-string wealth reset), C11
(fix the timezone-dependent clustering — a proof that differs by host is not a
proof), and C6 (give pinned edges a demotion path, or stop documenting pinning as
the preferred route).

**4. Rebuild the inference layer.** C10 first — the bootstrap is the foundation
every other statistic sits on. Then C13 and C15: deduplicate the hypothesis space
on executable geometry (which also fixes H2), and stop counting zero-trade
candidates. Re-derive the floors from the power you actually need rather than
round numbers.

**5. Reset the economics as one system.** C2, H1, H3. The 30 bps stop floor, the
25 bps authorizing stress, the 1.0 cost-to-risk limit, and the 25% notional cap
jointly determine whether any edge can survive. Choose them against a target net
expectancy, not independently.

**6. Then the safety and execution defects.** C9, H6-H11, M4, M5.

**Before any of it: write the null test.** Feed the gates a zero-edge random walk
and assert nothing validates. It is the cheapest item on this list and it would
have caught most of C10-C15 years earlier. Add the power test alongside it, and
make at least one test import the real broker SDK types.
