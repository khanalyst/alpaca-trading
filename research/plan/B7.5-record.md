# B7.5 — maker-first entry (H-K ii)

**Status: shipped, off by default, not yet validated against a live account.**

This document previously recorded why the integration was withheld. The
reason was real - a passive fill created a position, and the bookkeeping that
makes a position safe lived inside `open_position()` where only the IOC path
could reach it. That has now been fixed by extraction rather than by
duplication, so this section records what changed and what is still owed.

## Why the hypothesis is worth testing

Every IOC entry crosses the spread and accepts adverse selection: you buy at
the moment a seller wants to sell to you. At a 2% stop, round-trip friction
is roughly 10% of the risk unit, so converting the filled fraction from taker
to maker moves expectancy by more than most of the parameter axes queued for
sweeping.

It also matters disproportionately for a reason unrelated to its size: **it
does not require any signal to have edge.** It improves whatever edge exists
and removes a constant drag from the measurement of every other hypothesis.

Fill rate cannot be inferred from history, because the passive order was
never there. This is a live experiment and nothing else.

## What shipped

`Exchange.maker_first_entry()` — posts a post-only limit joining the near
touch, polls for a fill until a bounded deadline, and returns the fill rate,
the realised average, and the realised price against the IOC counterfactual.

Its one hard guarantee: **the order is never left resting.** Every exit
either reports a fill or has cancelled. When the cancel fails the fill state
is re-read rather than assumed, because assuming unfilled would double the
position on a later cross and assuming filled would leave one unmanaged.
Protection is attached to the passive order exactly as the IOC path attaches
it, so a fill arrives already protected.

`execution.maker_first_enabled` (default `false`) and
`execution.maker_first_wait_seconds` (default 20, bounded to 120). The wait
is bounded well inside a 15m signal bar: a passive order that outlives its
bar is being filled on a setup that no longer exists.

17 tests cover the fill-reporting paths, the cancel-race, post-only, attached
protection, and the counterfactual arithmetic.

## How the integration was made safe

Two extractions, both behaviour-preserving and both verified by the existing
safety suite passing unchanged.

`Exchange.settle_fill()` — reads the live position, runs `ensure_protection`,
alerts on an unverified stop, and attaches the mark and liquidation prices.
`open_position()` and `maker_first_entry()` both call it.

`Engine._settle_entry()` — the trade journal row, the liquidation-distance
check, the state bookkeeping reconciliation depends on. Both entry paths in
`_execute_open` call it.

So a passive fill now runs **identical** bookkeeping to an IOC fill. That is
the property that matters, and it is asserted directly:
`SharedSettlementTests` reads the source of both paths and requires each to
delegate.

The first attempt at this did not do the extraction. It returned early on a
passive fill and would have created a position with no journal row, no
liquidation check and no protection audit. It was reverted rather than
patched. Recording that is the point of this file.

### Failure behaviour

| Outcome | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Disabled | The exchange is never called |
| Unfilled | Falls through and crosses, exactly as before |
| Attempt raised | Falls through and crosses; the error is journalled |
| Cancel failed, order may rest | **Stops.** Crossing on top of a live order could double the position |
| Filled | Settled, then through `_settle_entry` like any fill |

### The partial-fill policy, chosen

**A partial passive fill is not topped up by crossing the remainder.**
Splitting one setup across two prices makes the recorded entry an average of
two decisions, and entry price is exactly what H-K(ii) measures. The position
is smaller than planned, which is journalled, and that is the conservative
direction. Written down here rather than defaulted into.

## What is still owed

**It has never touched a real exchange.** Every test runs against a `Mock`.
That proves the logic and proves nothing about OKX's actual post-only
semantics, partial-fill reporting, or cancel races under load. The plan says
the safety suite is this batch's gate, and the safety suite cannot exercise a
passive order.

So before enabling `execution.maker_first_enabled`:

1. Run it on the demo account and confirm on real fills that protection is
   attached and no order is left resting.
2. Have gate G2 passing first, so a working replay can detect any change in
   decision behaviour.
3. Then measure fill rate at T against the IOC counterfactual - the
   measurement the whole hypothesis exists to produce.

## Prerequisite that is already satisfied

The plan gates this behind batch 6 so the change-control process is exercised
on something lower-risk first. Batch 6 has shipped, with its replay diff
published. That gate is met — this one is held on a different constraint.
