# B7.5 — maker-first entry (H-K ii): shipped in half, deliberately

**Status: the exchange primitive ships. The entry-path integration does
not.** This document exists because an unexplained omission looks like an
oversight, and this one is a decision.

## Why the hypothesis is worth testing

Every IOC entry crosses the spread and accepts adverse selection: you buy at
the moment a seller wants to sell to you. At a 2% stop, round-trip friction
is roughly 10% of the risk unit, so converting the filled fraction from taker
to maker moves expectancy by more than most of the parameter axes queued for
sweeping.

It also matters disproportionately for a reason unrelated to its size: **it
does not require any signal to have edge.** It improves whatever edge exists
and removes a constant drag from the measurement of every other hypothesis.

Fill rate cannot be inferred from history, because the passive order was
never there. This is a live experiment and nothing else.

## What shipped

`Exchange.maker_first_entry()` — posts a post-only limit joining the near
touch, polls for a fill until a bounded deadline, and returns the fill rate,
the realised average, and the realised price against the IOC counterfactual.

Its one hard guarantee: **the order is never left resting.** Every exit
either reports a fill or has cancelled. When the cancel fails the fill state
is re-read rather than assumed, because assuming unfilled would double the
position on a later cross and assuming filled would leave one unmanaged.
Protection is attached to the passive order exactly as the IOC path attaches
it, so a fill arrives already protected.

`execution.maker_first_enabled` (default `false`) and
`execution.maker_first_wait_seconds` (default 20, bounded to 120). The wait
is bounded well inside a 15m signal bar: a passive order that outlives its
bar is being filled on a setup that no longer exists.

17 tests cover the fill-reporting paths, the cancel-race, post-only, attached
protection, and the counterfactual arithmetic.

## What did not ship, and why

**The engine does not call it.** `_execute_open` is byte-for-byte what it was
before this batch, apart from a comment.

The reason is the other half of an entry. A passive fill creates a position,
and every position this engine opens must also receive a `trade_id`, a
journalled trade row, a liquidation-distance check, and a protection audit
confirming the exchange-side stop actually exists. That bookkeeping currently
lives inside and around `open_position()`.

A first attempt at wiring returned early on a passive fill — and would have
created **a position with no journal row and no verified protection**. That
is a far worse outcome than paying the spread. It was reverted rather than
patched, because the patch is not small: doing it properly means extracting
the post-fill verification so both paths share it, and that is a refactor of
the code whose entire job is to guarantee no position is ever naked.

**It cannot be validated against mocks.** The plan says the safety suite is
the gate for this batch, and the safety suite cannot exercise a real passive
order. Everything above is tested against a `Mock` exchange, which proves the
logic and proves nothing about OKX's actual post-only semantics, partial-fill
reporting, or cancel races under load.

`tests/research/test_maker_first.py::NotWiredTests` pins the absence, so
anyone who wires it later gets a failing test pointing at this document
rather than discovering the requirement in production.

## What finishing it requires

1. Extract the post-fill path from `open_position()` — trade journalling,
   liquidation check, protection audit — into something both entry paths
   call.
2. Decide the partial-fill policy explicitly. Crossing the remainder splits
   one setup across two prices, which makes the recorded entry an average of
   two decisions and corrupts the measurement H-K(ii) is about. Abandoning
   the remainder gives a smaller position than planned. Neither is obviously
   right; it should be chosen and written down rather than defaulted into.
3. Exercise it on the demo account and confirm, on real fills, that
   protection is attached and no order is left resting.
4. Only then enable it, and measure fill rate at T against the counterfactual.

## Prerequisite that is already satisfied

The plan gates this behind batch 6 so the change-control process is exercised
on something lower-risk first. Batch 6 has shipped, with its replay diff
published. That gate is met — this one is held on a different constraint.
